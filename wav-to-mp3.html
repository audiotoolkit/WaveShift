WAV to MP3 guide

How to convert WAV to MP3 online

To make an MP3 copy, add your WAV file to the converter above. Choose a bitrate and select Convert audio. When the conversion is complete, download the new MP3 file. Your original WAV file is not changed.

  1. Choose one or more WAV files from your device.
  2. Choose an MP3 bitrate: 128, 192, 256 or 320 kbps.
  3. Select Convert audio, then download the new MP3 file.

The right bitrate depends on what you are converting. For speech, 128 kbps may be enough. For most music, try 192 or 256 kbps. Choose 320 kbps if you want a higher bitrate and do not mind a larger file. The best setting can vary with the recording.

Why convert WAV to MP3?

WAV files can take up a lot of storage space. An MP3 copy is usually smaller and easier to email, upload or share. MP3 also works on most phones, computers, music players and websites.

Keep your original WAV file if you may need to edit it later. MP3 removes some audio data to make the file smaller. Converting an MP3 back to WAV will not restore the data that was removed. Try not to convert the same file to MP3 again and again.

Common reasons to make an MP3 copy

  • Smaller uploads: reduce the amount of data sent to a website, learning platform, or messaging service.
  • Portable listening: create a compact file for phones, cars, and older media players.
  • Podcast drafts: share a review copy with collaborators before delivering a final master.
  • Storage: keep a smaller listening copy while retaining the original WAV for editing.

Which MP3 bitrate should you choose?

Bitrate tells you how much data an MP3 uses each second. A higher bitrate usually gives you a larger file and keeps more of the source detail. It cannot improve a poor recording or add sound details that are not in the original WAV. Choose a setting based on how you will use the MP3.

How much smaller will the MP3 be?

The file size depends mainly on the bitrate and the length of the recording. As a guide, 128 kbps uses about 1 MB per minute, 192 kbps uses about 1.4 MB per minute, and 320 kbps uses about 2.4 MB per minute. The final size may vary slightly.

WAV and MP3: which one should you keep?

WAV is often used for original recordings and editing because it can store uncompressed sound. MP3 is smaller and easier to share. Keep the WAV file if you may edit the recording again. Use MP3 when you need a smaller file for listening or sharing.

Recommended workflow: edit and archive from the highest-quality original you have. Export a separate MP3 for delivery. Avoid treating a converted MP3 as a replacement master.

Does WAV to MP3 conversion work offline?

You need an internet connection the first time you use the tool because the conversion engine must be downloaded. After it loads, the audio is converted in your browser. Your WAV file is not sent to a WaveShift server.

ⓘQuality reminder: Converting a file changes how audio is stored. It cannot recreate information that was removed during an earlier lossy encode.

People also ask

Here are simple answers to common questions about WAV to MP3 conversion.

What is the best bitrate for converting WAV to MP3?

For everyday music, try 192 or 256 kbps. For speech or smaller files, 128 kbps may be enough. Choose 320 kbps if you want a higher bitrate and do not mind a larger file.

Does converting WAV to MP3 reduce quality?

Yes. MP3 makes files smaller by removing some audio data. A higher bitrate usually keeps more detail, but MP3 is still a lossy format.

How much smaller will my MP3 be?

The size mainly depends on the length of the recording and the bitrate. A one-minute MP3 at 192 kbps is about 1.4 MB. The exact size may vary.

Can I convert WAV to MP3 without uploading the file?

Yes. WaveShift converts the file in your browser. Your WAV file is not sent to a WaveShift conversion server.

Should I delete the WAV after converting it?

Keep the WAV if it is your original recording or master file. Use the MP3 as a smaller copy for listening or sharing.

Can I convert multiple WAV files at once?

Yes. Select or drop several WAV files. The tool converts them one at a time and gives each MP3 its own download.

Is 320 kbps MP3 better than WAV?

No. A 320 kbps MP3 is still compressed. WAV usually stores uncompressed audio. MP3 is smaller, while WAV is useful for editing and keeping an original copy.
