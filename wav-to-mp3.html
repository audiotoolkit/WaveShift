WAV to MP3 guide

How to convert WAV to MP3 online

To make an MP3 copy of a WAV recording, add the WAV file to the converter above and start the conversion. WaveShift encodes the audio as MP3 and lets you choose a bitrate. The converted copy appears below the progress indicator; download it to your device when the job is complete. Your original WAV is not overwritten.

  1. Choose one or more WAV files from your device.
  2. Select an MP3 bitrate: 128, 192, 256, or 320 kbps.
  3. Convert the audio and download the new MP3 file.

The best bitrate depends on the content and how the file will be used. For many spoken-word recordings, 128 kbps may be adequate. For general music, 192 or 256 kbps is a useful starting point. Choose 320 kbps when you prefer the highest common MP3 bitrate and accept a larger file. These are practical starting points, not guarantees that every listener or source will sound the same.

Why convert WAV to MP3?

WAV files are often much larger than compressed audio formats. If you recorded a long interview, exported a music mix, or received a PCM recording, making an MP3 copy can make it easier to email, upload, store, or play on a wider range of devices. MP3 remains a familiar format across phones, media players, websites, and many audio applications.

Keep the original WAV as your master file if you may need to edit or re-export it later. MP3 uses lossy compression, so each encode can discard some audio information. If you convert a WAV to MP3 and later convert that MP3 back to WAV, the result does not regain the original PCM data. Repeated lossy conversions should be avoided whenever possible.

Common reasons to make an MP3 copy

  • Smaller uploads: reduce the amount of data sent to a website, learning platform, or messaging service.
  • Portable listening: create a compact file for phones, cars, and older media players.
  • Podcast drafts: share a review copy with collaborators before delivering a final master.
  • Storage: keep a smaller listening copy while retaining the original WAV for editing.

Which MP3 bitrate should you choose?

Bitrate is the amount of encoded data used per second. For constant-bitrate MP3, a higher bitrate generally means a larger file and usually less aggressive compression. It does not turn an imperfect source into a perfect recording, and it cannot add detail that is not present in the WAV. Choose a setting based on the source and the purpose of the converted file.

How much smaller will the MP3 be?

For a fixed bitrate, the approximate audio size is determined by bitrate multiplied by duration. As a simple rule of thumb, 128 kbps uses around 1 MB per minute, 192 kbps around 1.4 MB per minute, and 320 kbps around 2.4 MB per minute, before small container differences. Actual results depend on the source length, channel layout, encoder settings, and metadata.

WAV and MP3: which one should you keep?

WAV is commonly used for uncompressed PCM audio and is a practical choice for master recordings, editing, and production hand-offs. MP3 is a lossy delivery format designed to reduce file size. If you expect to edit the audio again, retain the original WAV. If you need a convenient copy for listening or sharing, MP3 is often easier to distribute.

Recommended workflow: edit and archive from the highest-quality original you have. Export a separate MP3 for delivery. Avoid treating a converted MP3 as a replacement master.

Does WAV to MP3 conversion work offline?

The conversion engine must be downloaded the first time you use the tool, so an initial internet connection is needed. Once the engine is loaded in the current page session, the actual audio processing is performed locally in your browser. The selected WAV file is not sent to a WaveShift server.

ⓘQuality reminder: Converting a file changes how audio is stored. It cannot recreate information that was removed during an earlier lossy encode.

People also ask

These are practical answers to the questions people commonly have before converting this type of audio.

What is the best bitrate for converting WAV to MP3?

For everyday music, 192 or 256 kbps is a sensible starting point. For speech or smaller files, 128 kbps may be enough. Choose 320 kbps when you want the highest common MP3 bitrate and do not mind a larger file.

Does converting WAV to MP3 reduce quality?

Yes. MP3 is a lossy format, so encoding WAV to MP3 discards some audio information. A higher bitrate generally reduces the amount of compression, but MP3 is still lossy.

How much smaller will my MP3 be?

Size depends mainly on duration and bitrate. At 192 kbps, a one-minute MP3 is roughly 1.4 MB before minor overhead. The exact result varies with the encoder and source.

Can I convert WAV to MP3 without uploading the file?

Yes. WaveShift uses FFmpeg WebAssembly in your browser. The tool reads and converts the file locally rather than sending it to a WaveShift conversion server.

Should I delete the WAV after converting it?

Keep the WAV if it is your original recording or master. MP3 is useful as a smaller listening or sharing copy, but it should not replace the highest-quality source if you may need to edit or export again.

Can I convert multiple WAV files at once?

Yes. Select multiple WAV files or drag them into the upload area. They are processed sequentially and each finished MP3 gets its own download link.

Is 320 kbps MP3 better than WAV?

No. A 320 kbps MP3 is still lossy. WAV usually stores uncompressed PCM audio. The MP3 is smaller and convenient, while WAV is more suitable as an uncompressed working or master file.
